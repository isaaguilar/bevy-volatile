# Rulebook

1. **Turns and ingredient placement:** Each turn allows two actions in any combination: movement, ingredient placement, or pickup. Before completing the second action, click UNDO in your action column to reverse the first action and recover any energy or items spent. A placement chosen first is shown on its target but does not take effect until the second action or rest completes the turn; reactions and damage then use the players' final positions. Two placed items resolve in their chosen order and can react during the same turn. If the first item makes the second placement invalid, the first has already resolved and cannot be undone; the second is rejected without spending its item or energy, and you can choose another action or rest to finish. The bottom row shows how many actions remain. Placing an ingredient on your own tile or an adjacent tile, including diagonally, costs no energy. Placing one farther away costs the distance in cells minus one energy, even if it has no effect, except where placement is prohibited. Distance is the larger of the horizontal and vertical offsets, so diagonal steps count as one cell. A player cannot place an ingredient farther than their available energy allows; with five energy, a target can be up to six cells away. Energy costs are paid when the action is chosen, so the remaining energy limits the next action. Only Stone Dust can be placed in an existing hole, except when an earlier queued Stone Dust will fill it. When both actions are used without resting, any free walk, nearby placement, or pickup restores one energy total at the end of the turn, up to five. Energy-consuming jumps and distant placements do not restore energy. Failed actions, selections, cancellations, and hover toggles do not restore energy or consume an action.
2. **Tlaloc's Water:** Tlaloc's Water placed onto solid ground follows the costs in rule 1 and permanently turns the tile into water, unless Stone Dust or a fire explosion converts it back to solid ground as described in rules 14 and 6. Tlaloc's Water placed on a ridge remains there and does not change the terrain until it meets acid from Toad Wart; the placed item is then consumed and the ridge becomes solid ground. The reaction also occurs if the acid is already on the ridge, and the acid remains temporary. Tlaloc's Water cannot be placed on water or holes; attempting to do so displays a message and costs neither energy nor a turn. Its supply is unlimited, and it does not respawn as a field item.
3. **Storm Shard and water:** A Storm Shard placed onto water is consumed and triggers an immediate electrical pulse before the next player's turn. Tlaloc's Water placed onto a Storm Shard on solid ground creates water, consumes the shard, and triggers the same pulse. The pulse spreads through all connected water tiles, including diagonal connections. Each grounded player standing on that water loses one heart; a grounded player on a neighboring tile, including diagonally, loses half a heart once, even if adjacent to multiple electrified tiles. Hovering players are immune. A lighter flash marks neighboring tiles alongside the water flash. The pulse then dissipates and the water remains.
4. **Frost Crystal and water:** A Frost Crystal placed on water freezes that tile and all adjacent water tiles, including diagonals, without spreading farther. Tlaloc's Water placed onto a Frost Crystal on solid ground creates water and triggers the same freeze. Each grounded player on the affected water is frozen in place for their next two completed turns. A direct Frost Crystal hit on dry ground also freezes the player for their next two turns, even while hovering. Frozen players cannot move but can use items, pick up, or rest. Any grounded player ending their own turn on a frozen tile loses half a heart, whether or not they are frozen, including on the turn the tile freezes and after the player thaws. Being frozen does not cause additional damage. Ice damage is netted with healing and other damage. A Sunstone placed on a frozen player's tile thaws that player early; on frozen water it also thaws the tile, without steam or damage unless another reaction occurs. Players may place items on their own tile and undo a queued placement. Water ice otherwise remains until an explosion reaches it or its terrain is replaced; electricity does not thaw it. Hovering prevents water-based freezing and frozen-tile damage, but not a direct frost hit. Reapplying frost refreshes the applicable duration. A Frost Crystal used to freeze water or directly hit a player is consumed and respawns according to rule 11; one that misses on dry ground remains collectible. A direct Storm Shard hit still deals half a heart even while hovering and consumes the shard.
5. **Tlexochtli Ember:** Tlexochtli Ember forms a permanent separate layer on any tile except a hole. Adding ember to an existing layer does not create a second layer. Tlexochtli Ember remains until burned, including when water freezes or dries, unless the tile becomes a hole and its effects are cleared. The ember label reads "perm". Tlexochtli Ember does not change freezing or electricity. Sunstone and explosions ignite and consume ember as described in rules 6 and 13; other ember reactions are not yet defined.
6. **Sunstone, water, and ember:** Sunstone on unfrozen water without ember produces steam on the target and all eight adjacent tiles except ridges. Tlaloc's Water placed on a Sunstone on solid ground creates water and produces the same effect. Wind is chosen randomly from eight compass directions at the start and after each completed turn; the displayed direction applies to the current turn. The three leading tiles extend steam one additional tile downwind: a straight edge for cardinal winds or the corner and its two neighboring tiles for diagonal winds. Steam does not affect a ridge or extend through one at the leading edge. If a ridge blocks an extended tile, that stream instead reaches each in-bounds perpendicular tile that is not a ridge. Steam does not redirect again, and an off-board destination receives no steam. Anyone on the target immediately loses one heart; anyone on another affected tile loses half a heart once, including while hovering. The steam then dissipates. Water remains after steam. Sunstone on frozen water thaws it without steam, as described in rule 4. Sunstone on ember, or ember on a placed Sunstone, ignites and consumes all ember connected to that tile, including diagonally connected ember. Each ignited tile explodes once, dealing one heart to anyone on that tile and half a heart to anyone on an adjacent tile, including diagonals; overlapping explosions add their damage. An ember explosion centered on unfrozen water turns it into dry solid ground and clears its tile effects. Affected terrain-growth cells disappear without canceling the rest of the growth. Explosions thaw frozen tiles throughout their footprint, leaving the water intact for that chain of explosions. The explosions do not produce steam.
7. **Poison Frog Sphere:** A Poison Frog Sphere releases poison. Poison on solid ground or water remains on each affected tile for two completed turns, including the turn it arrives. Starting on the following turn, it spreads one tile per completed turn through connected unfrozen water or wet solid ground, including diagonals. Poison cannot be placed on or spread onto a frozen tile. Poison already on a tile when it freezes remains there without spreading, losing duration, or damaging players until after the tile thaws. Each application visits a tile at most once, so it cannot spread in circles; overlapping applications can extend contamination. Poison on dry ground does not spread. If Tlaloc's Water creates water on a poisoned dry tile before it clears, its remaining time pauses for that action and it can spread on the next completed turn. Anyone on an active poisoned tile at the end of a completed turn loses half a heart, including on the turn poison arrives; hovering players are immune, and overlapping poison does not increase this damage. Storm Shard has no special poison reaction, and electricity still conducts through poisoned water.
8. **Life Essence and health:** Life Essence placed on acid immediately removes the acid and its electrification, preserving the underlying terrain and other effects, then behaves normally on that terrain. Life Essence in water heals grounded players on that tile by half a heart at the end of each completed turn for three completed turns, including the turn it is added, for up to one and a half hearts. Both players' turns count; hovering players do not heal. Life Essence placed on dry solid ground or a ridge remains collectible, but activates when Tlaloc's Water creates water on solid ground or when combined with Thornheart, and cannot be picked up once active. Life Essence also reacts with Void Dust and Stone Dust as described in rules 17 and 18. The healing does not spread and ends early if the water is removed; it continues while frozen. Reapplying it refreshes the three-turn duration without stacking healing. All damage and healing during a turn are added together before applying the net change to each player, then health is limited to zero through five hearts. A player at zero can still receive healing; elimination has not yet been defined.
9. **Inventory and actions:** Each player starts with one Sunstone, one Life Essence, and unlimited Tlexochtli Ember, Tlaloc's Water, Void Dust, and Stone Dust. There is no per-ingredient inventory limit. A queued limited ingredient is removed from the inventory immediately; undoing the placement or rejecting it during resolution returns it. Unlimited ingredients never run out and cannot be picked up. Placed ingredients remain on the board until removed or consumed by a reaction; Toad Wart and Poison Frog Sphere are consumed when used and their effects remain on the board. The pickup action and current tile are highlighted when an eligible ingredient is present and an action is available. Click PICK UP in your action column to collect all eligible ingredients on your current tile. Pickup uses one action and fails without consuming it when none is available. Click REST in your action column or press R to end the turn: resting before any action fully restores energy, while resting after one action restores two energy instead of the usual free-action recovery. Both are capped at five and apply the usual end-of-turn effects. Clicking REST also cancels a current selection.
10. **Magic and hover:** Each player starts with five magic, the maximum, displayed as a blue bar under energy. Clicking HOVER toggles it without ending the turn or changing magic. The player can then move, use an ingredient, pick up, or rest. Each completed turn while hovering costs one magic; each completed turn without hovering restores one magic, up to five. Only the acting player's magic changes. A shadow marks a hovering player. Grounded players can jump over holes, but not ridges; hovering players can cross ridges. Hovering allows movement onto ridges and holes, but an unsafe destination requires at least two magic before moving and at least two unoccupied adjacent walkable exits. With one magic remaining on an unsafe tile, the player must move to walkable ground rather than perform another action, unless frozen in place; in that case the last magic is retained until they can move. Hover turns off automatically at zero magic, after that turn's effects resolve, and cannot be turned off on an unsafe tile. Hovering prevents electrical water, poison, water-based freezing, frozen-tile damage, and Life Essence healing, but does not prevent direct Frost Crystal or Storm Shard hits, steam, explosion, or Thornheart damage.
11. **Ingredient pool and respawn:** The field starts with one Sunstone and one Life Essence. After every three completed player turns, up to three ingredients are added on eligible tiles, alternating which player completes the drop turn. The game introduces at most 64 limited ingredients in total, including the two initial field items and the four in players' inventories, and guarantees at least two of each limited ingredient type in the pool. New ingredients are chosen from types with fewer than two copies first; afterward their relative weights are Coatl's Egg 1, Aquatic Creature and Thornheart 2 each, and every other limited ingredient 4. Unlimited ingredients are excluded from random drops and respawns. Consumed limited ingredients become eligible to return five subsequent completed turns after use or their reaction; Life Essence used for healing or dry Thornheart activation begins its delay after that effect ends, and Thornheart begins its delay after its last thorn disappears. Eligible recycled ingredients take priority over new ingredients and remain queued if no eligible tile is available. Once all 64 have been introduced, only recycled ingredients can spawn. Each spawn chooses a random unoccupied, dry solid tile without ember, acid, poison, thorns, or a placed item; missed turns do not accumulate extra spawns.
12. **Thornheart:** Thornheart and Life Essence activate when placed together, in either order. The base is the first thorn tile on the activation turn; for the remaining turns, the vine grows one adjacent tile per completed turn, randomly choosing between two neighboring compass directions selected at activation. A newly used Life Essence activates growth for seven completed turns; Thornheart placed on already active healing water uses the remainder of that seven-turn growth period, counted from when the Life Essence was activated. The vine continues after the three-turn healing effect ends. When the base is wet or natural water, a second vine grows from the base using the opposite directions. Drying pauses this second vine without removing it; rewetting resumes it while growth remains active. Growth crosses all terrain, but frozen tiles block that direction until thawed. At board edges, an available edge tile is used when the chosen directions cannot continue. After activation ends, each vine retracts one tile per completed turn from its tip toward the base; the base disappears after both vines have retracted. Players on thorns lose one heart per completed turn, even while hovering; overlapping thorns do not increase this damage, and healing is netted with it before health is applied. Dormant Thornheart is collectible, but active thorns are not. Any explosion stops further growth of a vine it touches and immediately removes the thorn tiles in its blast footprint; sections severed from the base disappear on the next completed turn. Remaining tiles keep their retraction slots: a blast neither skips destroyed tiles nor advances retraction, and each remaining active turn reserves a growth slot for the primary vine and, while the base is wet, the opposite vine.
13. **Obsidian:** Obsidian can be placed on any terrain except a hole, where placement is rejected without spending energy or a turn. Obsidian and Storm Shard placed together, in either order, produce an X-shaped explosion on the center and up to two tiles away in each diagonal direction. Obsidian and Sunstone produce a cross-shaped explosion on the center and up to two tiles away in each cardinal direction. If Obsidian contacts both Storm Shard and Sunstone at once, both patterns occur and their damage stacks at the center; all three ingredients are consumed. Electricity reaching Obsidian through connected water also causes the X-shaped explosion, but does not duplicate an X already triggered by the same Storm Shard. These explosions deal one and a half hearts to each player on an affected tile, including hovering players; the electrical pulse also deals its usual damage to grounded players in the water. Obsidian in any explosion's footprint detonates once in the same cross-shaped pattern as a Sunstone reaction. An Obsidian blast starting off a ridge stops before the first ridge in each direction; neither that ridge tile nor tiles behind it are affected. A blast starting on a ridge reaches its full range, including across other ridges. Each explosion can ignite ember or other Obsidian in its footprint, producing a chain reaction; overlapping blasts add their damage and all damage and healing are netted before health is applied. Explosion footprints are clipped to the board and affect Thornheart. Stone Dust placed on Obsidian is consumed with the Obsidian in an explosion on the center and eight adjacent tiles, dealing one heart at the center and half a heart on adjacent tiles, including to hovering players. Like the Void Dust explosion, it flattens ridges and can chain into ember and other Obsidian. After the blast, the center and four cardinal neighbors become ridges, replacing water or ice there. Consumed limited ingredients respawn according to rule 11.
14. **Stone Dust:** Stone Dust placed on water, including wet solid ground or frozen water, is consumed and converts the tile to dry solid ground, unless it reacts with Life Essence as described in rule 18. Stone Dust cannot be placed on an untainted dry solid tile or ridge unless Obsidian or Life Essence is present. It clears poison on the targeted tile, including on ridges. Stone Dust placed in a hole is consumed and fills it with solid ground. It creates a cross of ridges with Obsidian and grows ridges with Life Essence, as described in rules 13 and 18. Stone Dust does not cancel Void Dust. Stone Dust has no special reaction to Sunstone or wind. Tlaloc's Water and Stone Dust have unlimited supplies and do not respawn as field items.
15. **Toad Wart:** Toad Wart releases acid. Acid on solid ground or a ridge lasts five completed turns, including the turn it is applied; acid on water is permanent. Tlaloc's Water turns temporary acid on solid ground into permanent acidic water. Toad Wart cannot be placed in a hole. Grounded players on acid lose half a heart per completed turn, even if it is frozen; hovering prevents this damage. Acid has no special reaction to Obsidian or Frost Crystal. Life Essence removes acid as described in rule 8. Acid dissolves a dormant Thornheart or an entire active vine it touches; Thornheart cannot be placed on acid, and attempting to do so costs neither the item, energy, nor a turn. Stone Dust placed on acid is consumed and makes the acid temporary; Stone Dust on acidic water also converts the underlying water to solid ground. A Storm Shard and acid electrify the acidic tile in either order, consuming the shard. Electrified acid lasts five completed turns, including the reaction turn, and deals an additional one heart per turn to grounded players; normal electrical water pulses still apply. The shard and Thornheart respawn according to rule 11.
16. **Acid and poison:** Placing acid on active poison or poison on acid produces an X on the center tile and the four diagonally adjacent tiles, clipped to the board and excluding holes. Each affected tile receives acid and poison, except that frozen tiles do not receive poison. Acid and poison deal a combined one heart per turn while both are active. Acid permanently taints water in the X, and poison on water spreads through connected water according to rule 7. Other acid reactions apply on tiles reached by the X.
17. **Void Dust:** Void Dust placed on solid ground, including wet solid ground, creates a hole, while Void Dust placed on a ridge levels it to solid ground. On natural water, it can only be placed when it reacts with Obsidian or Life Essence. Void Dust and Obsidian cause an explosion on the center and all eight adjacent tiles, dealing one heart at the center and half a heart on adjacent tiles, including to hovering players. The explosion flattens ridges in its footprint and can chain into ember and Obsidian; afterward the center and its four cardinal neighbors become holes. Void Dust and Life Essence produce a growth from the center: one arm grows by one tile per completed turn for two turns, followed by an opposite arm for two turns. On the following turn both arms become holes; the center remains unchanged. Directions are chosen like Thornheart, with an edge fallback. Combining Void Dust with Obsidian or Life Essence consumes both placed ingredients; on solid ground, Void Dust placed first immediately makes a hole, so the other ingredient must already be there. Newly created holes clear tile effects and remove placed items; limited ingredients respawn according to rule 11, and active Thornheart vines touching them dissolve. Void Dust has no special reaction to Sunstone, Frost Crystal, Stone Dust, Tlaloc's Water, Storm Shard, ember, Toad Wart, Poison Frog Sphere, or Coatl's Egg.
18. **Stone Dust and Life Essence:** Stone Dust and Life Essence, including active Life Essence in water, consume both placed ingredients and start the same growth pattern and timing described in rule 17. Both arms become ridges, including when those tiles were holes or water; the center remains unchanged. Converted tiles lose water and ice, and acid on them becomes temporary. Life Essence respawns according to rule 11.
19. **Terrain safety:** Holes and ridges together can occupy at most 60% of the board, or 42 of its 70 tiles. Immediate holes and ridge-creating ingredient reactions that would exceed the limit are rejected without spending ingredients, energy, or a turn. Delayed growth creates holes or ridges only while space remains under the limit; any blocked tiles remain unchanged. When a new hole or ridge appears under a player, the player remains in place and automatically hovers if they have enough magic to remain hovering after the current turn; a player already hovering continues to do so if they can sustain it. Automatic hovering does not require the two adjacent safe exits needed when voluntarily moving onto unsafe terrain. The acting player needs at least two magic because hovering costs one on their turn; the other player needs at least one. If they cannot sustain hover, they move to the nearest unoccupied solid ground or water tile. Adjacent tiles, including diagonals, are considered first, followed by successively larger rings. Terrain effects on the destination apply normally.
20. **Coatl's Egg:** Coatl's Egg and Life Essence, including active healing water, combine in either order to summon a three-cell serpent. Both ingredients are consumed and respawn according to rule 11. Starting on the activation turn, it moves up to two cardinal cells after each completed player turn, taking a shortest route around its own body; it can move into the cell its tail vacates. Its steps and impacts are animated between turns, and the next player cannot act until the animation finishes. It targets the player with the most health, recalculating each turn; if health is tied, it chooses the closest reachable player, with player one breaking an exact tie. If no route exists, it waits. It follows its direct route even through hazards: contact with acid or a hole kills it. The first player its head reaches, including a hovering player or someone other than its intended target, takes the hit and the serpent disappears. An unpoisoned hit removes one and a half hearts but cannot reduce a living player below half a heart. If any part of the serpent touched active poison during the hit turn or the preceding completed turn, the hit instead reduces the struck player to zero hearts. Frozen poison is inactive. A poisoned serpent's head is purple.
21. **Aquatic Creature:** An Aquatic Creature and Life Essence combine in either order on water, including active healing water, consuming both ingredients. If both are already on dry solid ground, adding water also activates them. They respawn according to rule 11. Starting on the activation turn, four arms grow in a cross, each extending one cell per completed turn for three turns. Growth crosses holes but an arm stops permanently at a ridge or the board edge. On the next two completed turns, the whole arms rotate clockwise around the origin, sweeping one eighth of a turn each time. Rotation is animated from the base toward the tips between turns, and players cannot act until the animation and any Coatl animation finish. Every arm segment levels ridges it sweeps across into solid ground and removes collectibles in its path, even if its arm was blocked during growth; segments stop at the board edge. Removed limited ingredients return according to rule 11; unlimited items do not respawn. Contact with a creature deals one heart during each growth turn or one and a half hearts during each rotation turn, including to hovering players. A creature hits each player at most once per turn, even if they touch several segments or move away before the turn ends; different creatures can each hit. Damage from sweeping applies to every tile the arms cross, not just their final positions. Undone movement does not count as contact. The final position remains visible until the next completed turn, when the creature disappears without causing another hit.
22. **Rain and earthquakes:** Rain occurs after a randomly chosen 10 to 20 completed player turns, and the next interval is chosen independently each time. If fewer than 33% of board tiles are water, rain turns one eligible tile into permanent water. Earthquakes occur independently after 20 to 30 completed player turns and create up to two randomly chosen ridges or holes. Events use unoccupied dry solid tiles without items, tile effects, creatures, or active terrain growth, so they do not directly hit players or trigger ingredient reactions. Earthquakes respect the 60% unsafe-terrain limit and do not sever existing walkable connections. An event that cannot make a change still resets its timer. Event results are shown in the bottom status text.
